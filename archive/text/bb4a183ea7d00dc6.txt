ASEAN and China were unable to conclude negotiations on a long-delayed South China Sea code of conduct under the Philippines' chairmanship, despite reaching provisional agreement on about 80 percent of the draft, Manila's foreign minister said on Sunday.
hinese Premier Li Qiang on Monday committed during a meeting with Southeast Asian leaders to an effective, substantive and early conclusion of a code of conduct for the South China Sea, the Philippines' foreign minister said.
Negotiations on an ASEAN-China Code of Conduct in the South China Sea have dragged on for over two decades, but the Philippines, as current chair, had committed to speed up those talks and conclude the code this year.
Philippine Foreign Secretary Theresa Lazaro, who had previously said negotiations were "80 percent complete", was quoted by Reuters as saying that she trusted "that Singapore will be able to finish the discussions" next year.
Beijing claims most of the South China Sea despite an international ruling that its assertion has no legal basis, and its coast guard has clashed repeatedly with that of the Philippines.
The row has reared its head at inopportune times over the Philippines' year in ASEAN's rotating chairmanship, with a July foreign ministers summit opening less than a day after Chinese and Philippine sailors engaged in a clash.
Analysts say the disagreements are not only between ASEAN and China.
Collin Koh, a senior fellow at Singapore's S. Rajaratnam School of International Studies, said divisions among ASEAN members, including over the geographical scope of the code, had also slowed negotiations.
"Other than Beijing, intramural divergences within ASEAN also play a significant role," he said.
Koh said the remaining differences would be difficult to resolve and would require "major concessions" from the various parties.
Singapore will inherit the task of trying to secure agreement on the code of conduct when it assumes the ASEAN chairmanship next year.
