MANILA, Philippines – The Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) and China will not meet their deadline to conclude negotiations for the long-delayed Code of Conduct (COC) in the South China Sea under the chairmanship of the Philippines in 2026.
On Sunday, November 15, Philippine Foreign Secretary Maria Theresa Lazaro said ASEAN and China have reached provisional agreement on over 80% of the draft text.
“The negotiations now enter their final phase and we commend the dedication and constructive engagement of all delegations,” Lazaro said in a briefing on the opening day of the 49th ASEAN Summit and Related Summits.
“The parties agree to continue to work constructively with a view to concluding the negotiations on an effective and substantive Code of Conduct at the earliest possible time,” she said, reading from the detailed briefing statement.
The proposed COC is aimed at setting rules for ASEAN and China to follow to reduce tensions in the South China Sea.
China and several ASEAN member states, including the Philippines, have overlapping claims in the resource-rich waterway.
The pending milestone issues are whether the COC will be legally binding, its geographic scope, and how terms would be defined in the code.
‘More effective provisions’
A provisional agreement was reached regarding the relationship between the non-binding 2002 Declaration on the Conduct of Parties in the South China Sea (DOC) and the proposed COC.
Lazaro said the parties affirmed that the existing DOC will provide the foundation for the proposed COC.
The COC will also set out “more effective and substantive provisions” to guide conduct in the South China Sea.
“The parties now agree that the Code of Conduct, in addition to guiding conduct, will serve as a platform for practical maritime cooperation in the South China Sea,” Lazaro also said, adding that maritime cooperation would be on a “voluntary basis” and would respect “the sovereignty, sovereign rights, and jurisdiction of coastal states.”
In addition, they agreed that “the COC is intended to serve as a mechanism for the constructive management of differences at sea, and not to settle disputes,” according to Lazaro.
Not enough time
In 2023, ASEAN foreign ministers set 2026 as the deadline for the conclusion of talks on the proposed COC.
With negotiations on crucial issues still not completed, Lazaro sought to explain some of the “sensitivities,” such as potential ratification on the part of certain ASEAN member states in order to make the proposed document legally binding.
“I think there was not enough time. If we were given probably another five to six months, palagay ko (I think) we can finish that,” Lazaro said of the talks.
As ASEAN chair in 2026, the Philippines initiated nine rounds of negotiations and two senior officials’ meetings for the COC.
Lazaro said the proposed text was only at 50% before the country’s shepherding.
“Konti na lang pag-uusapan nila sa Singapore (They have few details left to talk about in Singapore),” she also said, adding that she trusts Singapore will be able to finish the discussions.
Singapore will assume the ASEAN chairmanship in 2027. – Rappler.com
