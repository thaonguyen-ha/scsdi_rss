Failure to finalize South China Sea code was expected – expert
MANILA, Philippines – The failure to finalize the South China Sea Code of Conduct (COC) was expected given several thorny “milestone issues” that remain difficult to resolve, maritime law expert Jay Batongbacal said on Monday.
Batongbacal said he had earlier assessed that there was a “big chance” negotiations would not be concluded during the Philippines’ chairship of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (Asean), despite Manila’s push to complete the binding agreement under its leadership.
“It’s not unexpected, and I think even within the different ministries of the different countries, they also were aware of that possibility. So it’s not surprising,” he said at the Manila Dialogue on the South China Sea.

“I would expect that Asean will continue to try to move the process forward next year,” he said, though noting that “the same obstacles will still be there… what you call the milestone issues or threshold issues that… are most difficult to resolve will still be there.”
On Sunday, Foreign Affairs Secretary Ma. Theresa Lazaro said that negotiators had made substantial progress but remained divided on key issues, including the code’s geographic scope, definition of terms and whether it would be legally binding.
She said the parties had reached provisional agreement on about 80 percent of the draft, up from roughly 50 percent when the Philippines assumed the Asean chairmanship.
