MANILA – Malacañang on Wednesday said it would not prevent Philippine officials from defending the country’s territorial rights and interests in the West Philippine Sea (WPS), while stressing the importance of resolving differences with China through diplomacy.
Presidential Communications Office Undersecretary and Palace Press Officer Claire Castro said diplomatic engagement with other countries is separate from the mandate of Philippine leaders to protect the country’s interests in the disputed waters.
“Mas maganda po na lahat ng pakikipag-usap sa anumang bansa ay dapat nadadaan sa diplomasya pero hindi natin mapipigilan ang iba nating mga liderato na ang mandato ay ipagtanggol ang ating karapatan at ang interes natin sa West Philippine Sea (It is certainly better for all engagements with any nation to be conducted through diplomacy; however, we cannot restrain some of our leaders whose mandate is to defend our rights and interests in the West Philippine Sea),” Castro said in a Palace briefing.
Her remarks came amid reports of a phone conversation between Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi and Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) Secretary Ma. Theresa Lazaro, during which the two supposedly agreed to responsibly manage their differences, stabilize the situation and expand areas of shared interest.
Wang reportedly urged the Philippines to rein in politicians who had repeatedly “smeared” Beijing and crossed its “red lines.”
The reported exchange came amid renewed tensions between Manila and Beijing over the South China Sea, including recent diplomatic exchanges and competing claims over the WPS.
Earlier in October, the DFA summoned Chinese Ambassador Jing Quan following his remarks that China would “easily win” a conflict with the Philippines. Some senators subsequently called for his expulsion over the statement.
China claims most of the South China Sea, including maritime features within the Philippines’ exclusive economic zone (EEZ). In 2016, an international arbitral tribunal invalidated the legal basis of Beijing’s expansive claims under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea.
Firm WPS stance
Meanwhile, Defense Secretary Gilberto Teodoro Jr. said the Marcos administration has adopted a firmer stance on the WPS dispute, recognizing the need for the country to strengthen its capabilities to assert and protect its sovereignty and people.
“The achievement of this administration is to come to grips with reality that we have to be stronger and assert ourselves more in order to protect our people more,” he said in a radio interview when asked about the administration’s accomplishments in the WPS.
Teodoro also urged future administrations not to abruptly change defense policies upon assuming office, warning that doing so could affect or derail existing plans.
He cited the Department of National Defense’s (DND) ongoing efforts to strengthen the Philippines’ control over its EEZ and continental shelves, with the help of the Armed Forces of the Philippines. (Darryl John Esguerra and Priam Nepomuceno/PNA)
