UN chief lauds Asean’s progress in South China Sea COC
MANILA, Philippines — United Nations (UN) Secretary-General Antonio Guterres on Monday lauded the Association of Southeast Asian Nation (Asean) member states for their progress in negotiations for the code of conduct (COC) in the South China Sea.
Guterres mentioned this in his opening remarks at the 16th Association of Southeast Asian Nations (Asean)-UN Summit held at the Philippine International Convention Center.
“I commend Asean’s efforts, including progress in negotiations on the Code of Conduct in the South China Sea,” Guterres said.
The parties to the COC had reached provisional agreement on about 80 percent of the draft, up from roughly 50 percent when Manila assumed the Asean chairmanship, according to Department of Foreign Affairs Secretary Ma. Theresa Lazaro.
Lazaro said negotiators had made substantial progress but remained divided on key issues, including the code’s geographic scope, definition of terms, and whether it would be legally binding.

As early as 1992, Asean called for a maritime code of conduct with Beijing, which is intended to guide the conduct of claimants and prevent incidents from escalating.Manila refers to parts of the South China Sea within its maritime zones as the West Philippine Sea.
Beijing claims sovereignty over almost the entire South China Sea, but the landmark 2016 international tribunal ruling in accordance with the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (Unclos), effectively dismissed this in favor of Manila’s sovereign rights.
In a press conference after the Asean-UN Summit, Guterres said the UN stands for Unclos.
This was his response when asked how it could preserve the dispute settlement mechanism when China, a signatory of Unclos, refuses to recognize the 2016 Arbitral Award which affirms the principle of the UN convention.
“We as the United Nations, of course, stands for the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea, and we consider it as very important to have a successful negotiation of the code of conduct that is being conducted at the present moment by Asean with China,” Guterres said.
