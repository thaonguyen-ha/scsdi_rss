Teodoro urges ‘peaceful, legal’ settlement of South China Sea row
LAPU-LAPU CITY, Cebu — Defense Secretary Gilberto Teodoro Jr. on Wednesday urged Southeast Asian nations to resolve South China Sea disputes through peaceful and legal means amid shifting regional security conditions.
Teodoro made the call as defense leaders gathered in Lapu-Lapu City for the 20th ASEAN Defence Ministers’ Meeting (ADMM) and the 13th ADMM-Plus on Oct. 28 to 29, 2026 to discuss shared security concerns.
“ASEAN was founded on the belief that our strength lies in our diversity. We must therefore not allow our differences to divide us from within,” Teodoro said in his opening remarks.

“And where such differences exist, including in the South China Sea, we shall settle them through peaceful and legal means,” he added.
Maritime security, underwater infrastructure
Teodoro called for stronger cooperation on maritime security and the protection of critical underwater infrastructure, saying more work was needed to address these concerns.
This comes amid issues tied to the South China Sea, which is subject to overlapping claims involving China, the Philippines, Brunei, Malaysia, Vietnam, and Taiwan.

Under the 1982 United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), countries’ maritime rights and entitlements are governed by international law.
In 2016, an arbitral tribunal ruled that China’s claims to historic rights within its nine-dash line had no legal effect to the extent they exceeded the maritime entitlements allowed under UNCLOS.
Earlier maritime cooperation efforts
Teodoro’s remarks follow ASEAN leaders’ approval of a proposal for the Philippines to host an ASEAN Maritime Center during the 48th ASEAN Summit in Cebu in May 2026.
The proposed center aims to coordinate maritime operations among ASEAN’s 11 member-states and promote freedom of navigation, maritime safety, and peaceful transit.
President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. said the initiative was not intended to “confront” any specific country or power.
The summit also produced an ASEAN declaration on maritime cooperation reaffirming commitments to peaceful dispute resolution and international law.

Beyond maritime disputes
Beyond maritime disputes, Teodoro urged ASEAN members to expand cooperation on food and energy security amid supply chain disruptions caused by conflicts outside the region.
He also cited the looming super El Niño as a regional security concern, warning of potential effects on food, water, electricity, and connectivity.
The defense secretary called for frank discussions among defense leaders on regional threats, including during bilateral meetings, while respecting national policies and diplomatic considerations.
On tensions between Cambodia and Thailand, Teodoro welcomed efforts to maintain their ceasefire and pursue a peaceful, lasting settlement.
“For our part, the Philippines reaffirms its continued participation in the process settled by our leaders, the ASEAN observer team, with due regard for the sensitivities of all concerned,” he said.
