AT A GLANCE
- The Philippines' Department of Foreign Affairs expressed serious concern over China's 'unlawful interference' during a humanitarian operation for Filipino fisherfolk at Scarborough Shoal, following a collision between two Chinese vessels.
- The incident involved a China Coast Guard vessel and a People's Liberation Army-Navy destroyer chasing the Philippine Coast Guard's BRP Suluan, resulting in damage to the Chinese vessels and the use of water cannons against Philippine ships.
- Despite China's claims of territorial intrusion, the Philippines remains committed to defending its rights in the West Philippine Sea and will continue to engage in diplomatic efforts to manage the situation peacefully.
MANILA, Philippines — A day after two Chinese vessels collided in the West Philippine Sea, the Philippines’ Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) on Tuesday, August 12, said it was “seriously concerned” by China’s “unlawful interference” in a humanitarian operation for Filipino fisherfolk in Scarborough Shoal.
On August 11, a China Coast Guard (CCG) vessel collided with a People’s Liberation Army-Navy destroyer as both were trying to chase down the Philippine Coast Guard (PCG)’s BRP Suluan some 10.5 nautical miles away from Scarborough Shoal (Bajo de Masinloc).
Footage from the PCG from before the collision showed the CCG vessel chasing the BRP Suluan at full speed when the Chinese Navy ship also sailed between the two smaller white ships. The same footage showed extensive damage on the CCG vessel, as well as scratches on the gray Chinese navy ship.
“The Department is seriously concerned by the dangerous maneuvers of two vessels of the People’s Liberation Army Navy (PLAN) and the China Coast Guard (CCG), and their unlawful interference with a routine humanitarian operation for Filipino fisherfolk in and around the territorial sea of Bajo de Masinloc, which is a longstanding and integral part of Philippine territory,” the DFA said in a statement.
“Their actions not only posed a grave danger to Philippine personnel and vessels, but also resulted in the unfortunate collision between the two Chinese vessels. Conscious of its obligations to render assistance in times of distress at sea and its mandate under its jurisdiction, the Philippines without hesitation offered medical aid and other relevant support to the Chinese side, including towing the damaged China Coast Guard vessel out of the area to ensure the safety of navigation for other vessels,” it added.
China also used watercannons against both the PCG and the Bureau of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources.
The CCG, however, did not respond to the PCG’s offer.
“Yesterday’s incident demonstrates the importance of adhering to international maritime rules such as the 1972 International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGS) and the 1974 Safety of Life at Sea Convention (SOLAS). The Philippines has repeatedly emphasized the importance of maritime safety, and is prepared to work with relevant parties to draw lessons from this incident,” said the DFA.
China’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs, in a statement, claimed the Philippines “intruded” in the territorial waters and air space of Huangyan Dao, their name for Scarborough Shoal. The shoal, a high-tide elevation with its own territorial waters, is within the Philippines’ exclusive economic zone (EEZ) but has been controlled by China since 2012.
Chinese maritime forces — whether the CCG, Chinese navy, or the so-called Chinese Maritime militia — routinely employ dangerous manuevers in the West Philippine Sea, particularly against Philippine ships. Beijing claims almost all of the South China Sea, including areas that are part of the Philippines’ EEZ, an area that’s part of what Manila calls the West Philippine Sea.
China’s claim and refusal to recognize the 2016 Arbitral Award has meant that in the West Philippine Sea, Philippine vessels are subject to China’s aggressive actions. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., in a press conference on August 11, said Philippine ships would not pull out of the West Philippine Sea, despite harassment from China.
“We always continue to do the… Our uniformed personnel, the Navy, the Coast Guard, the Army, the Marines, all of them, the Air Force, all of them are pledged. They took an oath. And they’re just doing their duty. They’re performing their mission. Defend the Philippines. ‘Yun lang naman ang ginagawa nila (That’s all they are doing). And we will not stop doing it. We will not stop doing it. We will stop doing it when the threat stops,” said the Philippine president.
The DFA, in its August 12 statement, said it would continue to “[utilize] diplomacy and dialogue to address differences and produce positive outcomes, in line with the President’s instruction to manage the situation in the West Philippine Sea constructively and settle disputes peacefully” as it commended “the calm professionalism and world-class seamanship of the men and women” of the PCG.
The Suluan, BRP Teresa Magbanua, as well as the BFAR vessels were in the vicinity of Scarborough Shoal to help Filipino fisherfolk. – Rappler.com
