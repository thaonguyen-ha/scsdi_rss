Teodoro on South China Sea code: I’m not paid to trust an adversary
MANILA, Philippines – Defense Secretary Gilberto Teodoro Jr. on Monday declined to say whether he trusted Beijing to honor a proposed code of conduct (COC) in the South China Sea, saying he could not rely on the good faith of what he described as an “adversary.”
Asked at the Manila Dialogue on the South China Sea whether Beijing would uphold such an agreement, Teodoro said: “I am not paid to rely on the good faith of an adversary.”
“If I rely on the good faith of an adversary in my job as defense secretary, then they might as well find somebody else,” he added.
On Sunday, Foreign Affairs Secretary Ma. Theresa Lazaro said negotiators had made substantial progress but remained divided on key issues, including the code’s geographic scope, definition of terms and whether it would be legally binding.

She said the parties had reached a provisional agreement on about 80 percent of the draft, up from roughly 50 percent when the Philippines assumed the Asean chairmanship.
The lack of a finalized South China Sea code under the Philippines’ chairship was expected given a series of thorny “milestone issues” that remain difficult to resolve, maritime law expert Jay Batongbacal earlier said.
He had earlier assessed that there was a “big chance” negotiations over the COC would not be concluded this year, as Manila pushed to finalize the binding agreement under its leadership.

“It’s not unexpected, and I think even within the different ministries of the different countries, they also were aware of that possibility. So it’s not surprising,” he told the same forum.
“I would expect that Asean will continue to try to move the process forward next year,” he said, though noting that “the same obstacles will still be there… what you call the milestone issues or threshold issues that… are most difficult to resolve will still be there.”
Asean and China pledged in 2002 to come up with a COC on the South China Sea, a framework that seeks to prevent conflict through diplomatic means but it has remained elusive due to slow progress. /dl
