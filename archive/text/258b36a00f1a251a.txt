70% of South China Sea code agreed, key issues remain — DFA
MANILA, Philippines — China and the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (Asean) have agreed on about 70 percent of the proposed Code of Conduct (COC) in the South China Sea, but major issues remain unresolved as the year-end deadline for concluding negotiations approaches.
DFA Asean spokesperson Dominic Xavier Imperial said Thursday that the remaining 30 percent still covers major issues being discussed by negotiators.
Xavier declined to specify which provisions have yet to be settled.

“I would also like to stress that 70 percent of the COC package has already been agreed,” Imperial said during a briefing on the Asean political-security pillar at the opening of the International Media Center in Pasay City.
He described the progress as a significant achievement under the Philippines’ Asean chairmanship but could not say whether negotiations would be completed before the country’s term ends in December.
“I cannot really pre-empt how the COC negotiators will conclude this year,” he said.
He said the remaining provisions involve specific details in the draft agreement that negotiators are still working to resolve.
President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. earlier said he wanted the code of conduct to be finalized during the summit.

Foreign Secretary Ma. Theresa Lazaro earlier identified four outstanding issues: Whether the agreement would be legally binding, its geographic scope, its relationship with the 2002 Declaration on the Conduct of Parties in the South China Sea, and its terms of reference.
Imperial stressed that the proposed agreement should regulate the conduct of parties in the disputed waters while respecting the rights of countries outside the negotiations.
“The COC should be able to dictate the behaviour of all parties in the South China Sea,” he said.
He added that it should also “respect the rights of third parties and not just those negotiating the COC.”
Asean and China agreed in 2023 to accelerate negotiations and conclude the long-delayed code by 2026. Negotiators have been meeting monthly since January under the Philippine chairmanship.
The proposed code seeks to prevent maritime confrontations and manage tensions in the South China Sea, where Beijing’s sweeping territorial claims overlap with those of several Southeast Asian countries.
The negotiations have taken on greater urgency amid repeated confrontations between Philippine and Chinese vessels in the West Philippine Sea.
The 2016 arbitral ruling invalidated China’s expansive maritime claims in the South China Sea, but Beijing has refused to recognize the decision.
