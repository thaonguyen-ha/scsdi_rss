MANILA: Negotiations on the South China Sea Code of Conduct (COC) have reached a critical juncture, with Malaysia's Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim calling for renewed political will from all parties to conclude an effective and substantive agreement.
Anwar said Malaysia, as co-chair of the negotiations with China, remained committed to maintaining momentum towards concluding the long-awaited COC.
"Renewed political will from all delegations is imperative in achieving our shared goal of an effective and substantive COC,” he said in his national statement at the 29th Asean-China Summit here on Monday (Nov 16).
The talks, originally targeted for completion by the end of 2026, will now continue into 2027 under Singapore's Asean chairmanship. Philippine Foreign Secretary Ma. Theresa Lazaro said negotiators had reached provisional agreement on about 80 per cent of the paragraphs in the draft COC.
Asean and China signed the Declaration on the Conduct of Parties in the South China Sea (DOC) in 2002 to promote peaceful dispute settlement and regional stability. The proposed COC is intended to build on that declaration by establishing more substantive rules to manage tensions and prevent incidents in the disputed waters.
Delivering the Asean Common Statement on behalf of the regional bloc, Anwar said Asean welcomed the momentum and significant progress achieved in the negotiations.
"We look forward to sustained, intensive efforts towards the conclusion of an effective and substantive COC, in this final phase of the negotiations.
"We reaffirm the importance of maintaining peace, security, stability, safety and freedom of navigation in and overflight above the South China Sea,” he said.
Malaysia is the Asean country coordinator for dialogue relations with China.
Turning to economic cooperation, Anwar said the Asean-China Free Trade Area (ACFTA) 3.0 Upgrade Protocol came into force at the summit, marking a significant step towards strengthening economic ties between the two sides.
He said Malaysia welcomed cooperation with China to maximise the benefits of the upgraded ACFTA, particularly its provisions on small businesses, standards, competition and consumer protection.
He said Asean looked forward to closer cooperation with China in the digital economy and artificial intelligence (AI), including capacity-building efforts to make digital ecosystems more secure and resilient.
Anwar said Asean-China relations remained strong as both sides marked 35 years of dialogue relations and the fifth anniversary of their Comprehensive Strategic Partnership (CSP).
"Asean reaffirms its shared commitment to continue working closely with China to deepen and advance the Asean-China CSP in the years ahead,” he said.
He said Asean welcomed China's continued support for Asean centrality and Asean-led mechanisms, as well as its readiness to sign and ratify the Protocol to the Southeast Asia Nuclear Weapon-Free Zone Treaty without reservations.
"We appreciate China’s strong support in addressing non-traditional security challenges, including the increasing threats of cybercrime and online scams,” he said.
On West Asia, Anwar reiterated Malaysia's condemnation of atrocities against Palestinians, including what he described as genocide in Gaza.
"We continue to call for a just and peaceful resolution and an end to the atrocities committed. In this vein, we value China’s US$100mil contribution for the relief and reconstruction of Gaza,” he said.
Anwar is leading the Malaysian delegation to the 49th Asean Summit and Related Summits, hosted by the Philippines under the theme "Navigating Our Future, Together”.- Bernama
