MANILA – President Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. on Monday tasked newly installed Philippine Coast Guard (PCG) Commandant Admiral Rommel Supangan to build on his predecessor’s accomplishments as the country continues to face maritime challenges in the West Philippine Sea (WPS).
At the change-of-command and retirement ceremony for outgoing Commandant Admiral Ronnie Gil Gavan at South Harbor in Manila, Marcos expressed confidence that Supangan’s experience in maritime operations and leadership had prepared him for the post.
“I am confident that you will build on the accomplishments of your predecessor, further strengthening the Philippine Coast Guard with the full support of this administration,” he said.
The leadership transition comes as the PCG continues to assert Philippine maritime rights amid tensions with China in the WPS.
The PCG monitors Chinese maritime activities, assists Filipino fishermen, and protects Philippine interests in contested waters.
While Marcos did not announce any specific new directive on the WPS dispute during the ceremony, he underscored the Coast Guard’s role in protecting the country’s maritime interests and upholding sovereign rights.
“Across our waters, the Coast Guard works alongside other maritime agencies to safeguard our people, protect our maritime interests, and uphold our sovereign rights,” the President said.
Marcos credited Gavan’s three-year leadership with strengthening the PCG’s capacity to save lives at sea and respond to emergencies.
During Gavan’s tenure, the Coast Guard conducted nearly 5,000 search-and-rescue operations, rescuing 22,784 people, he said.
The agency also improved its emergency response capabilities through its 10/30 doctrine, which enables faster deployment of assistance when lives are at risk.
During Typhoon Tino and Super Typhoon Uwan in 2025, the PCG mobilized personnel and vessels for rescue and relief operations, including the preemptive evacuation of 630,812 people ahead of Uwan.
“Together, these achievements reflect a Coast Guard that is more capable, more responsive, and better prepared to serve our people,” Marcos said.
The President also recognized Gavan’s more than three decades of public service.
Gavan, a member of the Philippine Military Academy’s Maalab Class of 1993, received the Presidential Legion of Honor in recognition of his service.
Supangan previously served as commander of the Coast Guard Fleet, Coast Guard District Palawan and the Marine Environmental Protection Command.
Marcos urged PCG personnel to sustain the institution’s record of service as it marks 125 years since its founding.
“For 125 years, generations of Coast Guardians have answered the call to protect our people and our seas. That responsibility now rests in your hands,” he said. (Darryl John Esguerra/PNA)
