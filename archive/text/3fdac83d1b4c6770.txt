MANILA – The Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) and China must bring the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) at the core of the ongoing negotiations on a Code of Conduct in the South China Sea, UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said on Monday.
Speaking to reporters at a press conference in Pasay City, the UN chief said the oceans pact would be key in achieving a “successful” conclusion to the talks.
“The question is that, as you know, several countries do not recognize the Convention on the Law of the Sea. We, as United Nations, of course, stand for the UNCLOS, and we consider that it is very important to have a successful negotiation of the Code of Conduct that is being conducted at the present moment by ASEAN with China,” he said.
The UNCLOS is a legally binding agreement that defines the rights of a state over its maritime zones, including internal waters, territorial sea, the 200-nautical-mile exclusive economic zone, and the continental shelf.
ASEAN and China have completed 80 percent of negotiations on a COC, with negotiators expected to continue talks through 2027.
Foreign Affairs Secretary Ma. Theresa Lazaro earlier said the two parties agreed the COC will serve only as a “mechanism for the constructive management of differences at sea” and “not to settle disputes.”
Negotiators have so far settled the connection between COC and the 2002 Declaration of Conduct (DOC), one of the four outstanding issues in the talks.
Lazaro said the parties have also agreed to use the final COC as a “platform” for maritime cooperative activities in the South China Sea that are practical and in accordance with UNCLOS. (PNA)
