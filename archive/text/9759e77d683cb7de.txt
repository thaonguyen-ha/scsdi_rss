MANILA: Asean must engage directly with China to resolve disputes in the South China Sea and prevent external powers from exploiting delays in negotiations to interfere in the region, says Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim.
The Prime Minister said it was important for Asean and China to expedite negotiations on the South China Sea Code of Conduct (COC), as prolonged delays could create opportunities for other powers to intervene.
"I do not want other powers to interfere and exploit these gaps to make themselves feel that they have a role to play.
"It is better for the region to resolve this in the spirit of Asean through negotiations and continued progress," he told Malaysian media here on Monday (Nov 16) night.
Anwar said Asean must also ensure that the South China Sea does not become a battleground for major powers, stressing that direct engagement with Beijing was consistent with the principle of Asean centrality.
"We do not want this region and the South China Sea to become a battleground for major powers.
"Since this involves the South China Sea, it is better for Asean to negotiate directly with China and resolve the matter amicably," he said.
Anwar said he had discussed the matter with Chinese Premier Li Qiang during the 29th Asean-China Summit earlier in the day, including efforts to strengthen bilateral cooperation and advance negotiations on the COC.
He welcomed China's commitment towards establishing a long-term framework to address disputes in the South China Sea, particularly in safeguarding peace, freedom of passage and the safety of air and maritime transportation.
He said Beijing had responded positively to Asean's position, with Li reiterating China's commitment to advancing negotiations during the summit.
Asked why he had earlier described negotiations as being at a "critical juncture", Anwar said the matter had remained unresolved for too long, despite an earlier target to conclude negotiations by the end of this year.
However, he acknowledged that substantial progress had been made, with China demonstrating a more positive commitment towards reaching an agreement.
"I just want to strongly urge that there should be no further delays.
"It becomes critical because I fear that if negotiations continue to be delayed, other elements may find an opportunity to intervene," he said.
On whether Asean and China had agreed on a new deadline to conclude the COC, Anwar said no new timeline had been established.
"No, we did not. We only acknowledged the fact that there has been a lot of progress, and we have heard commitments from both Asean leaders and Premier Li Qiang," he said.
Anwar said negotiations remained sensitive because they involved overlapping territorial claims, although both sides had agreed in principle on the broader parameters of maintaining peace and ensuring freedom of passage.
He said the finer details would require further negotiations and that extending the timeline could be justified if meaningful progress continued.
"When there is clearly progress in these negotiations, we should not give this deadline that may frustrate the progress.
"If it's just a negotiation without any progress, then I can understand," he said.
Anwar said China's renewed commitment demonstrated that negotiations were moving in the right direction.
"There has been clear progress, and we will make sure that we can conclude this expeditiously," he added.
